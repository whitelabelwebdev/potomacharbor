AI Uncertainty Requires a New Approach to Workforce Planning

As AI reshapes jobs, skills, and how work gets done, organizations face growing uncertainty about their future workforce needs. Yet only 40% of organizations have a clear vision for their workforce over the next three to five years.

PotomacHarbor's inaugural study of 106 HR executives and senior leaders points to a new approach: shifting the focus of workforce planning from forecasting headcount to making faster, better workforce decisions, with explicit trade-offs as conditions change.

Organizations with stronger decision-ready workforce planning capabilities reported more than 4 percentage points higher earnings growth than their peers.

Key Research Findings

2% → 10%median share of the workforce

AI agents are becoming a significant part of the workforce. Respondents expect their median share of the workforce to grow from 2% today to 10% in three years.

76% / 33%experiment vs. redesign

AI experimentation is outpacing work redesign. While 76% report that their organizations embrace local AI experimentation, only 33% say they actively redesign jobs and workflows in response to AI and automation.

28%effective scenario modeling

Workforce planning isn’t keeping pace with uncertainty. Just 28% report effective workforce scenario modeling, even as uncertainty about AI’s impact emerges as a major planning barrier.

51% / 8%headcount vs. tasks

Traditional headcount planning still dominates. 51% of respondents primarily plan around headcount, compared with just 8% who focus on tasks and activities.

From Static Plans to Better Decisions: The 4S Framework

Our research points to the need for a more dynamic approach to workforce planning. PotomacHarbor's 4S Framework provides a practical way for organizations to make workforce decisions as business needs and technology evolve.

S

See

Understand the work, skills, and capabilities that drive business performance.

S

Sense

Anticipate changes in business needs, technology, and talent markets.

S

Simulate

Evaluate workforce options and trade-offs across different scenarios.

S

Shift

Act on decision triggers to redeploy talent, redesign work, and adjust workforce strategies.

Rather than seeking increasingly precise forecasts of an uncertain future, organizations can use this approach to make better decisions about how work gets done, who or what does it, and when to change course.
